# 06. Practitioner knowledge for prompting LLMs to write Korean fiction: vendor guidance, Korean practitioner advice, AI-style phrase lists, tools, and Korean prose style guides

Research date: 2026-10-17. Scope: practical, non-academic material only (academic prompting, figurative-language, vocabulary, story-generation and discourse papers are covered by other researchers). Source numbers [S#] refer to the source table at the end of the last section.

Evidence labels used below: **[V]** vendor documentation; **[M]** measured (benchmark data or corpus counts; who measured and on what domain is stated); **[T]** tool documentation or tool source code; **[P]** practitioner opinion or anecdote (unmeasured); **[SG]** style guide or language-authority material; **[N]** news reporting.

Important caveat on the Korean measurements: most Korean numbers in these notes come from the in-repo experiments of two open-source projects, im-not-ai [S18] and novel-copilot [S20]. They are preliminary, not peer-reviewed, and were measured mostly on essays, columns, blogs and wiki text (im-not-ai) or on the project's own generated web-novel chapters (novel-copilot). They are the best practical evidence found, not established findings, and they were not measured on literary/print fiction.

## 1. What do official vendor guides recommend for creative writing and long documents?

### Takeaway
Vendors converge on a common recipe: a one-sentence role, explicit and motivated instructions, 3 to 5 diverse examples in tags, long material placed first with the task last (Anthropic, Google) or instructions at both ends (OpenAI GPT-4.1), XML delimiters, positive rather than negative instructions, and a prompt written in the same style as the desired output. None of the vendors opened has a dedicated creative-writing guide; Anthropic's newest guidance explicitly warns against "mannered prose" (decorative metaphor), which creates a tension with WriterProgram's goal of fresh figurative language that must be resolved by defining figurative language as sparse and earned. NAVER (CLOVA Studio) and LG (EXAONE) publish no Korean creative-writing prompting guidance; LG only gives sampling parameters.

### Cited Findings

**Anthropic, Prompting best practices (current reference page; covers Claude Fable 5.1, Mythos 5.1, Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Haiku 5.5 and earlier) [V]**
- "Golden rule": show the prompt to a colleague with minimal context; "If they'd be confused, Claude will be too." Be specific about format and constraints. — [S1 Anthropic best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)
- Explain the motivation behind instructions; the doc contrasts "NEVER use ellipses" (less effective) with a version that explains the text-to-speech reason, adding "Claude is smart enough to generalize from the explanation." → Implication: each style rule in the fiction prompt should carry a one-clause reason (e.g., mobile reading, reader immersion). — [S1](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)
- Examples are "one of the most reliable ways to steer Claude's output format, tone, and structure"; make them relevant, diverse ("vary enough that Claude doesn't pick up unintended patterns") and wrapped in `<example>`/`<examples>` tags; "Include 3–5 examples for best results." → Implication: author-written exemplar passages are the main style lever, but they must vary (see the copying warnings from OpenAI and novel-copilot below). — [S1](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)
- XML tags reduce misinterpretation when a prompt mixes instructions, context, examples and inputs; nest documents as `<documents><document index="n">`. — [S1](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)
- Role: "Setting a role in the system prompt focuses Claude's behavior and tone for your use case. Even a single sentence makes a difference." — [S1](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)
- Long context (20k+ tokens): "Place your long documents and inputs near the top of your prompt, above your query, instructions, and examples"; "Queries at the end can improve response quality by up to 30 percent in tests, especially with complex, multidocument inputs"; wrap each document with `<document_content>` and `<source>`; "ask Claude to quote relevant parts of the documents first before carrying out its task." → Implication: story bible, character sheets and prior-chapter text go at the top in tagged blocks; the chapter task and style card go last; ask the model to first retrieve the relevant canon facts. — [S1](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)
- Format control: "Tell Claude what to do instead of what not to do" (instead of "Do not use markdown", say "Your response should be composed of smoothly flowing prose paragraphs."); "Match your prompt style to the desired output... removing markdown from your prompt can reduce the volume of markdown in the output." → Implication: write the fiction system prompt in clean Korean prose (no bullets/bold/em dashes inside rule text) and phrase rules positively. — [S1](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)
- Anthropic's own "AI slop" admission (frontend section): "You tend to converge toward generic, 'on distribution' outputs... You still tend to converge on common choices (Space Grotesk, for example) across generations." The sample prompt names the overused defaults to avoid and asks for "unexpected choices that feel genuinely designed for the context." → Implication: an analogous "literary aesthetics" block can tell the model it converges on average phrasing and ask for context-specific choices; but see the conflicting "pink elephant" evidence in Section 2 about naming banned phrases. — [S1](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)
- Communication style: latest models are "more direct and grounded", "more conversational", "less verbose"; Claude Opus 5 is an exception whose default responses run longer. — [S1](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)

**Anthropic, Prompting Claude Fable 5.1 (Claude's own writing-style guidance) [V]**
- "Claude Fable 5.1's writing has few stock phrases and little unexplained jargon. In some cases, though, its prose is denser... sentences run longer and there are fewer paragraph breaks." Recommended instruction: "Mannered prose substitutes metaphor and flourish for direct statement... The phrases exist to display the writer, not to convey the idea, and readers can tell... Metaphors drag in connotations the writer did not choose and cannot control. The fix is to say what you mean. When a literal phrase is available, use it." Short version: "Please remove all mannered prose." → Implication for fiction: the vendor's default anti-pattern is decorative metaphor; WriterProgram should not ask for "more metaphors" but for figurative language that is rare, concrete, tied to the point-of-view character, and never a substitute for a plain statement. Also watch paragraph density for web-novel output. — [S2 Prompting Claude Fable 5.1](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5-1)
- Formatting: Fable 5.1 formats less than earlier models; remove old anti-formatting blocks; "In conversational, personal, or emotional exchanges, keep to plain prose." — [S2](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5-1)
- Long outputs at `xhigh`/`max` effort: the model may draft the whole deliverable in thinking and then write it again; run at `high`, set `max_tokens` with room, and append a supplied note saying that composing the full output twice "would double the length of the turn without improving the result." → Implication: for full-chapter generation, default to `high` effort and include that note when using higher effort. — [S2](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5-1)

**OpenAI, GPT-4.1 Prompting Guide (cookbook; archived; references model gpt-4.1-2025-04-14) [V]**
- Long context: "ideally place your instructions at both the beginning and end of the provided context"; if only once, "above the provided context works better than below." Delimiters: "XML performed well in our long context testing", "JSON performed particularly poorly." — [S3 OpenAI GPT-4.1 guide](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/gpt4-1_prompting_guide)
- Literal instruction following; "a single sentence firmly and unequivocally clarifying your desired behavior is almost always sufficient"; with conflicting instructions the model "tends to follow the one closer to the end of the prompt." — [S3](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/gpt4-1_prompting_guide)
- Copying risk: "When provided sample phrases, models can use those quotes verbatim and start to sound repetitive to users"; instruct variation ("never repeat a sample phrase in the same conversation"). Also: "ensure that any important behavior demonstrated in your examples are also cited in your rules." No all-caps or bribes needed. → Implication: never put sample sentences you do not want reproduced into rules; rotate exemplars; restate exemplar behaviors as explicit rules. — [S3](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/gpt4-1_prompting_guide)
- Suggested skeleton: Role and Objective / Instructions / Reasoning Steps / Output Format / Examples / Context / Final instructions. — [S3](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/gpt4-1_prompting_guide)

**OpenAI, GPT-5 Prompting Guide (cookbook; archived; no date on page, GPT-5 era 2025) [V]**
- Contradictory or vague prompts are "more damaging" because the model "expends reasoning tokens searching for a way to reconcile the contradictions"; removing contradictions "drastically streamlined and improved" performance. — [S4 OpenAI GPT-5 guide](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/gpt-5/gpt-5_prompting_guide)
- Metaprompting: ask the model "what specific phrases could be added to, or deleted from, this prompt" while keeping "as much of the existing prompt intact as possible." — [S4](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/gpt-5/gpt-5_prompting_guide)
- Format adherence "can degrade over the course of a long conversation"; consistent results came from "appending a Markdown instruction every 3-5 user messages." → Implication: in long serial-writing sessions, re-inject the style card periodically rather than relying on the first system prompt. — [S4](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/gpt-5/gpt-5_prompting_guide)
- Neither OpenAI guide contains creative-writing-specific prompting advice (the GPT-5 guide's closest material is code and UI aesthetics). — [S3](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/gpt4-1_prompting_guide); [S4](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/gpt-5/gpt-5_prompting_guide)

**Google, Gemini API "Prompt design strategies" (last updated 2026-10-13) [V]**
- "We recommend to always include few-shot examples in your prompts"; "if you include too many examples, the model may start to overfit the response to the examples"; keep example formatting consistent. Only positive demonstrations are shown (no anti-pattern examples). — [S5 Google prompt design strategies](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/prompting-strategies)
- Put persona, essential behavioral constraints and output format at the start of the system instruction; supply context first and put the question/instructions at the very end, linked by a phrase like "Based on the information above". — [S5](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/prompting-strategies)
- Break complex tasks into components and chain prompts; prefixes can start the response pattern. — [S5](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/prompting-strategies)
- Sampling: Gemini 4 and Gemini 3.6+ ignore custom temperature/top_p/top_k; earlier Gemini 3 models should keep defaults. → Implication: diversity cannot be bought with temperature on current Gemini; it must come from prompt design (rotating exemplars, opening variety, explicit rhythm rules). — [S5](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/prompting-strategies)

**LG AI Research, EXAONE 4.0 32B model card (paper arXiv 2507.11407, 2025-07-15) [V]**
- Non-reasoning mode: "we recommend using a lower temperature value such as `temperature<0.6`"; reasoning mode: `temperature=0.6`, `top_p=0.95`; "If you suffer from the model degeneration, we recommend using `presence_penalty=1.5`"; for the 1.2B model, "For Korean general conversation with 1.2B model, we suggest to use `temperature=0.1` to avoid code switching." No system-prompt or creative-writing guidance; benchmarks do not include creative writing. → Implication: if a Korean open model is used, repetition control (presence penalty) and code-switching risk at higher temperatures must be tested; low temperature may flatten prose variety. — [S6 EXAONE 4.0 model card](https://huggingface.co/LGAI-EXAONE/EXAONE-4.0-32B)

**NAVER Cloud, CLOVA Studio playground documentation (last updated 2026-09-17) [V]**
- The playground page describes only the editor (system instruction plus user/assistant turns, token counting, code view); parameters are documented on a separate page; there is no prompt-writing or creative-generation guidance on the page. — [S7 CLOVA Studio playground](https://guide.ncloud-docs.com/docs/clovastudio-playground)

### Inferences
- The vendor recipe maps cleanly onto a fiction prompt: tagged long material at the top (bible, character/호칭 table, recent chapter text), then rotating exemplars, then a positive style card with reasons, then the chapter task last; for literal-following models also restate the 3 to 5 hardest constraints at the top.
- Three vendors independently warn that examples and sample phrases get copied (Anthropic "vary", Google "overfit", OpenAI "verbatim"). Combined with novel-copilot's measurements (Section 2), this argues for rotating exemplar slots and for keeping fixed sample sentences out of rules.
- "Mannered prose" (S2) plus EQ-Bench's criteria against "gratuitous metaphor or poetic overload" (S9) and Surge's "over-metaphor and purple prose" finding (S13) all point the same way: the prompt should ask for fewer, better images, not more. "Fresh figurative language" should be operationalized as one concrete image at a decisive moment, drawn from the POV character's world, with plain statement elsewhere.
- Periodic re-injection of the style card (S4) is cheap insurance for long serial sessions.

### Gaps
- No vendor guide opened has creative-writing-specific prompting guidance; OpenAI's guides are archived. I did not open Anthropic's Korean-language docs page or any Anthropic page specifically about role-play/character consistency beyond the role section.
- No NAVER HyperCLOVA X prompt-engineering guide (system prompt advice, recommended parameters for creative generation) was found; the CLOVA parameter page (clovastudio-info) was not opened. Model lineup details (HCX-007, HCX-005, HCX-DASH-002) appeared only in a search snippet and were not verified.
- No LG creative-writing or Korean-style guidance exists beyond sampling parameters in the model card.

## 2. What do Korean practitioners recommend for getting natural Korean fiction from LLMs?

### Takeaway
Korean blog/community advice is mostly generic and anecdotal: specify role, length, POV and style; work in stages (worldbuilding, synopsis, outline, episode); analyze an author sample to define style; let the human polish emotion and logic. The most valuable Korean practitioner knowledge sits in open-source code, where authors recorded measured failures: naming a banned sentence pattern in the prompt increased it, em dashes inside rule text increased dashes in output, fixed example sentences were copied, a rigid paragraph quota produced "walls", and a static narrator card produced tics. Several popular rules (use more 의성어·의태어, use more metaphors) conflict with other practitioners and with vendor guidance.

**Open-source Korean tools whose code records practitioner experiments**
- novel-copilot (kariseio, GitHub; dated design notes 2026-07-15 to 2026-08-20) [T, in-repo measurements on its own generated chapters]. Default system persona: "너는 카카오페이지·네이버시리즈·문피아 유료 연재로 써 온 한국 웹소설 프로 작가다. 모바일 세로 화면으로 읽는 독자를 위해, 인물의 대사와 행동으로 장면을 끌고 가는 본문을 쓴다..." — [S20 novel-copilot](https://github.com/kariseio/novel-copilot)
  - Design rule: a fixed-size "양성 원칙 헌법" (8 positive rules); detectable axes (word tics, tense leaks, fragmentation, reused hooks) are handled by deterministic quality gates (`word_tics`, `tense_leak`, `fragmentation_score`) instead of the prompt; "새 'AI 티'는 규칙을 +1 하지 말고(부정 명령은 패턴을 소환·증식)". → Implication: separate prompt (positive gestalt rules) from linter (detectable patterns). — [S20](https://github.com/kariseio/novel-copilot)
  - Pink-elephant measurement (SR-1, 2026-07-15): a rule that named the banned example ("'A가 아니라 B였다'식 … 아껴 써라") "그 문형을 오히려 유발(라이브 1화 실측: 부정-대조 7건/5.2천자)"; fixed by deleting the named example and giving a positive direction ("직진 단언 + 동작 진행으로 긴 문장"). → Implication: banned-phrase lists belong in the linter, not in the generation prompt. — [S20](https://github.com/kariseio/novel-copilot)
  - Punctuation leak (EM-1, 2026-07-20): em dashes used inside the rule text acted as a few-shot anchor; dashes in output rose per chapter ("괴담작 1→3→4→7건/화 실측"); "지시문의 구두법이 곧 few-shot 앵커". Rules were rewritten without dashes (onomatopoeia and cut-off dialogue dashes are exempt). — [S20](https://github.com/kariseio/novel-copilot)
  - Example copying: "고정 예문을 넣지 않는다(예문은 모델이 골격째 베끼는 자기표절의 진원 → few-shot 앵커는 별도 회전 슬롯에서만)". — [S20](https://github.com/kariseio/novel-copilot)
  - Paragraph quota backfire (VP-1, 2026-08-13): the prescription "대체로 1~2문장" produced a "2문장 문단 벽" (author reading plus a 4-persona panel); replaced with 1 to 4 sentence variation (5 sentences judged the mobile drop-off point). — [S20](https://github.com/kariseio/novel-copilot)
  - Trade-off measured: fragmentation versus "-다" endings correlate at r = −0.95, so tightening fragments without an ending-variation rule produces a "-다 벽". — [S20](https://github.com/kariseio/novel-copilot)
  - Narrator voice (DP-17): the root of the "~했다 벽" is a narrator acting as an attitude-free camera; a `narrator_voice` field (attitude, verbal habits, what the narrator enjoys or mocks) is inserted for first-person works; generic style "quality" instructions were found null ("문체 '품질' 지시는 null(D-26~30)") while persona/author-style steering was effective ("조향 실측 유효"). → Implication: steer with identity (persona, narrator voice, author style), not with adjectives like "아름답게". — [S20](https://github.com/kariseio/novel-copilot)
  - Static voice card tics (VB-1): one static voice card across 30 chapters made the same reaction slot fill the same way; voice cards are now staged by protagonist state. — [S20](https://github.com/kariseio/novel-copilot)
  - Opening sameness (OP-1): "오프닝 동형 9/11 실측 + 독자 증언('시작이 틀로 찍은 듯 비슷')"; each chapter draws a positively phrased opening move from a pool excluding the previous chapter's type. — [S20](https://github.com/kariseio/novel-copilot)

### Inferences
- Recurring Korean advice, with quality: specify POV/tense/length/style (S20, S21, S26; consistent, partly measured in S20); avoid 번역투 (S18, S20, S21; S20 calls its 번역투 floor rule "E-02 실측 입증"); give or analyze author style samples (S24, S21, plus Sudowrite S14; anecdotal but consistent with vendor few-shot guidance); human revision of emotion and logic (S25, S26, S27, S30; anecdotal).
- Conflicts to resolve by testing: S21 says "비유·은유 적극 사용" while S20 and S2 say use them sparingly; S20 says "의성어·의태어는... 아껴 써라" while the commonly repeated advice to use rich 의성어·의태어 was not found in any opened source. A reasonable synthesis is "deliberate and earned, at decisive moments, never as filler".
- Write the system prompt in Korean prose whose punctuation and rhythm match the target (S1 "match prompt style", S20 EM-1). Both Korean tools write their prompts in Korean; I found no controlled comparison of Korean versus English prompts.
- Use positive rules for gestalt qualities in the prompt and deterministic gates for countable tells (S20), then a targeted revision loop (S18).

### Gaps
- Full text of Korean how-to books was not accessible (the 『챗GPT로 웹소설 쓰기』 ebook body; 『AI 2024』 prompts were images in S27).
- Korean web-novel writer communities (문피아 토론, 디시 웹소설 연재 갤러리, 아카라이브 AI 채널) did not yield retrievable posts with concrete phrase lists or presets.
- No source gives evidence on "write the prompt in Korean versus English" for Korean fiction quality.

## 3. What lists of "AI-sounding" phrasing circulate, in Korean and English, and what do quantitative analyses show?

### Takeaway
English lists are mature and quantified: the antislop data has 2,500 phrases and 2,000 words with frequencies plus regexes, the Antislop paper reports some patterns over 1,000 times more frequent in LLM output than in human text, and Wikipedia tracks how "AI vocabulary" drifts by model era. Korean lists are emerging: im-not-ai's taxonomy (10 categories, 85 sub-patterns, severity tiers) includes measured signals such as comma after connective endings (4.84×), negative parallelism (9.2×), absence of 100+ character sentences (11×) and generative conceptual metaphor (4.3×), but these were measured on expository text. Measurement also overturns some popular Korean rules: humans use "~것이다" about twice as often as AI in essays, and humans use more pronouns overall, while AI's distinctive problem is pronouns with no antecedent (6×). No Korean fiction-specific cliché list with frequencies (e.g., "심장이 쿵 내려앉았다") was found anywhere; it has to be built by translating English fiction slop and validating it on Korean corpora.

- Evidence status of the Korean candidates named in the assignment: "~에 대해" is backed (S18 A-1 S1 for expository text; S21 rule; S34 style guide); em dash overuse is backed (S18 J-3; S20 measured dash growth; S12); "그녀는" is backed only as a condition (antecedent-less or wall-to-wall use), not as a frequency cap (S18 A-16 shows humans use more pronouns overall; S38 and S37 for literary and historical context); "~하는 것이었다" has no fiction measurement and essays show "~것이다" is human-typical (S18 I-1, S19), so it should be observed, not banned; "마치 ~처럼" has only specimen evidence (S25) plus the general metaphor-overuse findings (S18 D-14, S2, S13); "심장이 쿵 내려앉았다" and "숨을 들이켰다" are plausible translations of top English slop items (S10) but unmeasured in Korean; "입술을 깨물었다" has no AI-specific evidence.
- Linter design implied by S18 and S11: density thresholds per 1,000 어절 or per chapter, severity tiers, co-occurrence scoring, a keep-one rule for rhetorical devices, exclusion of dialogue and quoted text, author voice-profile overrides, and a check that revisions never increase a tell count.
- Because tells drift (S12) and are model-specific (S11), WriterProgram should run its own antislop-style mining: generate Korean fiction with its production models from the same prompts, compare n-gram frequencies against a human Korean fiction corpus by genre (web novel genres versus literary fiction), and version the dictionary.

## 4. How do commercial and open-source tools structure fiction prompts?

### Takeaway
International tools separate three layers: persistent story knowledge (Sudowrite Story Bible, Novelcrafter Codex, NovelAI Lorebook with keyword-triggered insertion and token budgets), a short style field, and task-specific prompts (beat to prose, rewrite, describe by senses, summarize). Korean platforms (Kakao Entertainment, NAVER, Novelpia, 뤼튼) publish no author-facing generation prompt structures that I could find; Korean open-source projects (novel-copilot, tigerbookmaker, im-not-ai) are the best structural references, adding deterministic quality gates and revision loops that the international tools do not document.

## 5. Which principles from Korean prose style guides could become explicit prompt or lint rules?

### Takeaway
Classic guides converge on: avoid Japanese- and English-derived constructions ('-의' chains, '-적', nominalizing '것', plural '-들' on things or after 모든/여러, '가지다' for "have", '~에 의해' passives and other passives, '~로부터', '~에 있어서', '~와의/~으로서의' double particles, '~시키다/~화시키다', '이루어지다', '~아닐 수 없다', '-었었다'); prefer predicates and active voice; write as people speak (이오덕). '그녀' is a contested modern coinage (via Japanese 彼女), and novelist 김초엽 avoids it in fiction by using names. These rules are well suited to a linter, but measured data warns against blanket bans on forms humans also use (Section 3), and the guides target expository prose, so dialogue and deliberate character voice need exemptions.

### Cited Findings
- 김정선 『내 문장이 그렇게 이상한가요?』, summarized by 김하진 (브런치, 2026-04-21) [SG, secondary]: the "적·의·것·들" rules: drop '-적' when the sentence works without it ("사회적 현상 → 사회 현상", "경제적 문제 → 경제 문제"); drop '-의' when possession is unclear and rebuild around the predicate ("음악 취향의 형성 시기 → 음악 취향이 형성되는 시기", "부모와의 화해가 우선이다 → 부모와 화해하는 일이 우선이다"); turn "~하는 것이다" into "~한다" or a concrete noun (바, 일, 경우) or delete ("우리가 서로 알고 지낸 것은 어린 시절부터였다 → 우리는 어린 시절부터 서로 알고 지냈다", "상상하는 것은 즐거운 것이다 → 상상은 즐거운 일이다"); drop '-들' after 모든/여러 and on things ("모든 아이들이 손에 꽃들을 들고 → 모든 아이가 손에 꽃을 들고"). — [S33 김하진/김정선](https://brunch.co.kr/@elizabeth99/417)

### Inferences
- Prompt-side (positive) rules derivable from the guides: build sentences around predicates rather than noun phrases joined by '-의'; prefer active verbs with a clear human agent; refer to characters by name, 호칭 or zero anaphora, using a pronoun only when a clear antecedent precedes it; write sentences that read naturally aloud; keep possession and attributes in Korean patterns ("목소리가 아름답다" rather than "목소리를 가지고 있다").
- Linter-side rules: regexes for each construction above, scored by density, with exemptions for dialogue (characters may speak in 번역투 or bureaucratic Korean on purpose), for proper nouns and quotations, and for author voice profiles. Measured cautions from Section 3 apply: "~것이다" and "~를 통해" are common in human Korean, so flag clusters, not single hits.
- '-었었다' deserves a fiction-specific rule because past-perfect transfer appears in narration (이오덕 counted it in novels).

### Gaps
- I could not open the text of 이오덕 『우리글 바로쓰기』 itself (the wikidocs notes returned 403), 이수열's books, 김정선's book directly, or 국립국어원 『한눈에 알아보는 공공언어 바로 쓰기』; the rules above come from interviews, a summary, a news report and one 국립국어원 journal article.

- The prompt rules and linter entries reinforce each other: if a linter category fires often for a given model, strengthen the corresponding positive prompt rule (e.g., more long action-progression sentences when LX-04 fires) rather than adding a named ban (S20 SR-1).
- For literary/print fiction, relax the web-serial rules (ST-10 paragraphing, ST-14 cliffhangers, LX-07 dash threshold for authors who use dashes) through the author voice profile (S18 author-context pattern, S14 per-chapter style overrides).
- Validation plan implied by S11, S12 and S18: build paired corpora (same prompts, model output versus human Korean fiction by genre), compute per-entry density ratios, promote derived entries (LX-K*) to S2 only when the model-to-human ratio is clearly above 1 and human false positives are rare, and re-mine when production models change.

### Gaps
- None of the derived Korean entries (LX-K01 to LX-K28) has been measured; the Korean measured entries come from expository text, not fiction, and from non-peer-reviewed in-repo experiments.
- Thresholds for narration versus dialogue, and for web-serial versus literary fiction, are unknown and must be calibrated.
- No Korean vendor (NAVER, LG, Kakao) guidance on fiction prompting was found to validate or refine these rules for Korean-native models.
